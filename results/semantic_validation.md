# Semantic topic labels: checks (2026-11-20)

Generated by scripts/13b_semantic_validation.py. How the labels were made: data/semantic_labelling_prompt.md;
topic definitions: data/semantic_codebook_v2.md; labels: data/semantic_labels.csv.

## Stability

A random 8,536 papers were labelled a second time, in other batches by other agents. Agreement per topic
(Cohen's kappa) has median 0.96 and minimum 0.89 (Multi-task / transfer learning; Foundation models 0.89);
for the "main" labels median 0.94, minimum 0.72. 72% of these papers got exactly the
same "involves" topics both times (58% the same "involves" and "main"). This shows that the model is
consistent with itself, not that it is right.
Giving these papers their second-pass labels instead changes 0 of the 106 whole-period trend calls and 1 of the 2024-2026 calls: Image restoration / low-level vision (declining -> unclear, q 0.048 -> 0.058).

## Labels compared with the keyword rules

Keyword rules (data/topic_matrix_ta_keyword.csv): 261,030 paper-topic labels; semantic labels: 329,574; both: 206,852;
keyword only: 54,178; semantic only: 122,722. Per topic: results/semantic_vs_keyword_by_topic.csv.
Topics per paper (semantic, involves / main): 2020 3.02 / 1.68, 2021 3.17 / 1.75, 2022 3.28 / 1.79, 2023 3.55 / 1.86, 2024 3.85 / 1.94, 2025 4.19 / 2.02, 2026 4.62 / 2.10.
A paper has at most 3 "main" topics; the share of papers at that cap rose from 7.7% (2020) to 27.0% (2026), so the "main" table (results/shares_and_change_ta_main.csv) may drift down for
topics that often come with others.

## Blind re-judgement of the disagreements

2,004 cells were judged again without saying which method said "yes": 8 per topic for each direction of
disagreement, and up to 3 per topic where the two semantic passes disagree. Share of each verdict (weighted by stratum size):

| cells | involves, words used | involves, other words | passing mention | other sense of the word | absent | unsure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| keyword only | 16% | 5% | 41% | 25% | 11% | 2% |
| semantic only | 60% | 38% | 0% | 0% | 1% | 0% |
| the two passes differ | 40% | 48% | 2% | 3% | 3% | 3% |

Scored against this re-judgement (per topic: results/semantic_score_by_topic.csv; 8 cases per stratum, so a single
topic's numbers are rough: 8 of 8 right still has a 95% lower bound of about 0.63):
- by the labels' definition (a passing mention does not count): keyword rules precision 0.84, recall 0.64;
  semantic labels 0.99 and 0.96;
- by the keyword version's own definition (a passing mention counts): keyword rules 0.92 and 0.67; semantic
  labels 0.995 and 0.91.
The two versions answer different questions: how many papers mention a topic, and how many papers' own work involves it.

**What this does not show.** The re-judgement was made by the same model (Claude Opus 5.5) with the same codebook, so
none of these numbers is an independent error rate, and no human check has been made. The judge says "yes" more often
than a second labelling does: on the repeat papers, the second pass says yes on 6% of the first pass's keyword-only
cells (5,352) and on 91% of its semantic-only cells (12,291); the judge on 22% and 98% (weighted).
Where the two semantic passes disagree, the judge says yes about as often whichever pass said yes ("first pass no" means only the second pass said yes; keyword no, first pass no: 91% of 115; keyword no, first pass yes: 91% of 114; keyword yes, first pass no: 83% of 45; keyword yes, first pass yes: 79% of 34): either these cells are mostly members that each pass sometimes misses, or the judge leans to yes when unsure.
More limits:
- Cells both methods miss are invisible here. The repeat pass shows some (1,126 cells where the keyword rules and the
  first pass say no and the second pass says yes; 91% of the 115 re-judged ones are yes): at that rate at least about
  10,281 such cells in all papers, which caps recall at about 0.94 (semantic) and 0.63 (keyword).
- If the semantic-only cells were right less often than the re-judgement says, the semantic precision would be
  0.96 at 90%, 0.93 at 80%, 0.89 at 70%; if the keyword-only cells were half right, the keyword precision would be 0.90.

Yes-rate of the re-judged cells by period (weighted): keyword only 0.16 / 0.24 / 0.24; semantic only 0.99 / 0.99 / 0.98 (2020-22 / 2023-24 / 2025-26).
With 0-8 cases per topic and period, this cannot show whether a single topic's errors change over the years.

**Model.** All labels and re-judgements are Claude Opus 5.5's, except the last 50 papers of batch b617, which Claude Opus 5 (claude-opus-5) labelled after a model switch inside that agent.
Leaving those 50 papers out changes 0 of the 106 whole-period calls and 1 of the 2024-2026 calls: Image restoration / low-level vision (declining -> unclear, q 0.048 -> 0.050).
